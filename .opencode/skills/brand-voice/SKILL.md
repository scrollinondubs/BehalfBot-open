# Skill: Brand Voice - Sean / scrollinondubs

Use whenever writing blog stubs, social posts, or outreach emails.

Seeded from 10 posts on scrollinondubs.substack.com covering narrative, technical explainer, listicle, persuasive, showcase, syllabus, build-log, and manifesto formats. Ported from the Claude Code chassis skill on 2026-07-06.

## Voice Profile

### Openings

Bold declarations, personal anecdotes, or direct challenges. Never eases in.

- **Declaration:** "We are living in the future." - drops straight into the thesis
- **Anecdote:** Opens with his guitar teacher story, then pivots to the lesson about pedagogy
- **Challenge:** "If you do one thing this year, make it this."
- **Never:** "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." or any throat-clearing

### Sentence Rhythm and Paragraph Length

Short punchy sentences mixed with longer explanatory ones. Sentence fragments for emphasis. Paragraphs are 1-3 sentences, almost never more. White space is a feature, not a bug.

> "This stuff works. Not in theory. In Lisbon. Right now."

### Vocabulary and Register

Accessible but not dumbed down. Technical terms used naturally, never defined condescendingly. Playful naming conventions (Ralph Wiggum system, Confabulator). Pop culture references land because they're specific, not forced.

**Recurring terms:** "ship" (as verb), "leverage," "vibecode," "the thing is..."

### Structural Patterns

- Subheadings framed as questions the reader is already thinking ("So why doesn't everyone learn this?")
- "Why this matters:" as a framing device after setup
- Mini-narratives embedded in larger technical pieces
- Direct quotes from real people (students, builders) for social proof
- Lists used for scannability, not as a crutch
- **Convergence narratives** - connects past projects to current work to show a through-line. "The goal remains the same... the method has changed."
- **Concrete timelines as proof** - "Friday morning... Sunday afternoon" / "two days" / specific dates. Makes speed tangible, not abstract.
- **Real-company receipts** - when making a market case, uses specific company names plus exact numbers. Never "companies are laying off people."
- **Parenthetical asides** for quick clarifications and pricing: "(first section completely free - full course $69)"
- **Decision-narration moments** - when a tech choice is made, briefly tells the reader the alternative considered and rejected, before naming the choice. Makes the chosen path feel earned, not preachy, and demonstrates judgment.
- **Inline bolded keyphrase as quotable insight** - 2-3 per post, placed mid-paragraph (NOT as a header). Each is a self-contained statement someone could tweet.
- **Longitudinal self-receipts** - when staking a credibility claim, hyperlink older personal artifacts as proof. Not "I've done this before" but "here's the 17-year-old artifact."
- **Cost-first tech framing** - when introducing a technical capability, lead with the dollar figure rather than the feature. Makes the tech feel cheap-and-practical, not abstract.
- **Honest shortfall admission** - call out where you fell short of the prescribed thing, before the reader can question it. Buys trust and lets the win feel real, not hyped.
- **Three-takeaway structure** for retrospectives: a labeled but conversational takeaways block, each takeaway carrying its own bolded inline insight.

### Metaphors and Analogies

Extended metaphors as structural backbone - guitar teaching = AI pedagogy, playmaker vs quarterback for role framing. The metaphor does the explaining.

### Recurring Phrases and Verbal Fingerprints

- "This stuff works."
- "The tech has officially reached the point where..."
- "ship" as default verb for launching anything
- References to leverage and compounding
- "Here's the thing:" / "The problem is..." to pivot to the real point
- Self-deprecating humor about past failed projects
- "bang out" for rapid building
- "This is the way at this point." - casual Star Wars nods
- "double-whammy" for compound benefits
- "We are living in the future." as a mic-drop statement

### Tonal Range

Shifts fluidly between registers:
1. **Wry/playful** - default mode, especially intros and transitions
2. **Earnest/passionate** - student outcomes, community building, what's possible now
3. **Practical/authoritative** - tool recommendations, build logs, "here's exactly how"
4. **Manifesto/mission** - for big-picture posts. Urgency without panic. Backs claims with data.

More serious about student outcomes than almost anything else. Never cynical, never ironic about the work itself.

### What He Never Says

- Corporate speak ("synergy," "ecosystem," "leverage" as noun)
- Hedging ("it could be argued," "it depends," "some might say")
- Over-qualified statements with too many caveats
- Marketing buzzwords ("game-changer," "revolutionary," "unlock")
- Emoji-heavy prose (occasional emoji ok in social, never in long-form)
- "I" as first word of a piece - leads with the thing, not himself

### Closings and CTAs

Soft close, not hard sell. Three closing patterns:

1. **Next-thing tease:** Names what he's building or exploring next, invites the reader along.
2. **Thought exercise:** Extended "imagine forward" - asks the reader to picture their life with or without this skill.
3. **Personal mission statement:** States his purpose plainly and invites participation. "I look forward to meeting you."

CTAs are woven in naturally. Never "DON'T MISS OUT" energy.

## Headlines

- **Specific over vague:** "How I cut my build time in half" beats "A faster way to build"
- **Tension or contrast:** "The thing everyone gets wrong about..."
- **Short:** rarely more than 10 words
- **Declarative or provocative:** "You don't need to learn to code"
- **No clickbait:** the post always delivers what the headline promises

---

## For Outreach Emails

- Zero preamble - start with the relevant thing
- Never: "Hope this finds you well" / "I wanted to reach out"
- Reference something specific (their project, a meetup, a tool they used)
- One ask per email, stated plainly in the last paragraph
- Warm but direct - reads like a message from someone who respects your time
- Under 150 words
- Sign-off: "Sean" - no title, no Vibecode Lisboa in the signature

## Quick Reference: Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Open with a bold claim or story | Open with a generic observation |
| Use sentence fragments for punch | Write long compound sentences |
| Name specific tools, people, places | Stay abstract ("various tools") |
| Mix wry humor with earnest passion | Be uniformly sarcastic or serious |
| End with a question or next step | Summarize everything you just said |
| Use "ship," "build," "the thing is" | Use "leverage" (noun), "ecosystem" |
| Quote real people | Invent testimonials |
| Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences | Write wall-of-text paragraphs |
| Let the metaphor do the explaining | Over-explain after the metaphor |
